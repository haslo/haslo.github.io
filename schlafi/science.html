🧪 Actigraphy — The Science of Movement-Based Sleep Detection

Schlafi's sleep/wake detection isn't a novel invention. It's built on 40+ years of actigraphy research — clinically validated methods that infer sleep from movement patterns. The phone-on-mattress approach applies these same principles using the accelerometer already in your iPhone.

Research Basis

Actigraphy has been clinically validated since the 1980s. Wrist-worn accelerometers detect movement patterns that reliably distinguish sleep from wakefulness. The foundational algorithms — Sadeh (1994) and Cole-Kripke (1992) — remain the gold standard, with overall accuracy of 80–88% for binary sleep/wake classification.

A comprehensive 2023 review in Nature Digital Medicine ("40 years of actigraphy") found that classic regression-based algorithms perform as well as or better than modern machine learning approaches for sleep/wake detection. Simplicity wins.

How Phone-on-Mattress Works

When you place your phone on the mattress, body movements during sleep are transmitted through the mattress material to the phone's accelerometer. The signal is weaker than wrist-worn actigraphy (~9× attenuation) but the fundamental physics is the same: sleep produces less movement than wakefulness.

Mattress Transmission Hierarchy

Signal quality varies by mattress type. Spring mattresses transmit vibrations best (metal coils act as conductors). Latex mattresses offer moderate transmission. Memory foam absorbs the most vibration but still transmits enough for sleep/wake detection.

Schlafi uses relative measurements — comparing movement levels within a session rather than relying on absolute thresholds. This self-calibrates to any mattress type during the first ~30 minutes of a session.

Foundational Algorithms

Sadeh et al. (1994) developed a scoring index using an 11-minute sliding window with four variables: mean activity, number of active epochs, standard deviation, and log-transformed activity. Decision rule: SI > -4 → sleep. See Maths tab for the full formula.

Cole et al. (1992) used a weighted sum of activity counts in a 7-minute window centered on the current epoch. Decision rule: S < 1 → sleep. Both algorithms are implemented as simple arithmetic — no neural networks, no cloud.

🌌 Sleep Stage Classification from Phone Sensors

Beyond binary sleep/wake, Schlafi estimates sleep stages (REM, light, deep, awake) using movement patterns and sleep architecture timing. This is the Pillow approach: heuristic, not clinical, but useful.

Movement-Based Staging Approach

Movement patterns during sleep correlate loosely with sleep stages. The key insight: different stages produce different movement signatures.

🌑
Deep Sleep
Minimal movement. Muscle tone reduced but present. Longest sustained stillness periods. Predominates in first sleep cycles.
👁
REM Sleep
Muscle atonia (paralysis) with periodic twitches. Brief micro-movements distinguish it from deep sleep. Predominates in later cycles.
💤
Light Sleep
More movement than deep/REM. Position changes, restlessness. Transitions between other stages. Most time spent here.

The Pillow Approach

Apps like Pillow classify sleep stages from movement intensity combined with sleep architecture priors. Schlafi follows the same strategy:

  • Immobility duration discriminates deep vs REM — REM has periodic twitches while deep sleep is sustained stillness
  • Cycle timing provides a structural prior — sleep follows ~90-minute ultradian rhythms
  • Cycle position biases classification — first cycles have more deep sleep, later cycles have more REM (known sleep architecture)
  • Minimum bout duration prevents implausible fragmentations — no 30-second REM episodes
Validation: Honest About Limits

Lee T, Cho Y et al. (2023) conducted a multicenter study comparing 11 consumer sleep trackers against polysomnography. Phone-based apps achieved F1 scores of 0.26–0.69 for stage classification (vs 0.55–0.81 for wearables).

Staging from a phone accelerometer has fundamental limits. Without heart rate and HRV data, distinguishing REM from deep sleep relies on subtle movement differences that aren't always present. Schlafi is transparent about this:

  • Confidence indicators per epoch — low-confidence classifications are flagged
  • Aggregate night confidence shown as a data quality indicator
  • Watch data when available dramatically improves accuracy (heart rate + HRV add orthogonal signals)
What Schlafi Claims — and Doesn't
Supported

  • Stage timing patterns are real (ultradian rhythms)

🎙 Additional Sensor Signals

The accelerometer is the primary sensor, but iOS devices have additional sensors that can improve sleep tracking accuracy when the user opts in.

🎤

Microphone (Optional)

Breathing pattern detection and snoring identification. Adds a staging signal (regular breathing = sleep, irregular = light/awake). Privacy-sensitive — must be explicitly opt-in with clear data handling disclosure. Audio processed on-device, never uploaded.

🔊

Sonar Mode (Speaker + Mic)

Emits inaudible ultrasonic pulses from the speaker and listens for reflections. Detects chest movement (breathing) contactlessly from a nightstand. No mattress contact required. Similar to Google's Nest Hub sleep sensing approach.

📲

Proximity Sensor

Detects face-down placement for optimal mattress contact positioning. Can also detect when the phone is picked up (wake event). Low power, always available.

📱

Screen Interaction

Coarse bedtime/wake estimation from last screen lock and first screen unlock. Provides session boundaries even without continuous accelerometer recording. Zero additional permissions required.

Multi-Signal Fusion

Combining accelerometer data with optional microphone input provides better classification than either alone. The accelerometer detects movement; the microphone detects breathing regularity and rhythm. Together they address the primary weakness of movement-only staging: distinguishing REM from deep sleep (both have low movement, but breathing patterns differ).

⚙️ iOS Implementation Landscape

Key iOS frameworks and APIs that make phone-based sleep tracking possible.

CMSensorRecorder

Core Motion's background accelerometer recording. Records up to 12 hours of continuous data at 50 Hz. Data stored on-device and retrievable after the recording period. The foundation for overnight phone-on-mattress tracking.

HealthKit Sleep Analysis

HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis defines the stage vocabulary: inBed, asleepUnspecified, awake, asleepCore, asleepDeep, asleepREM. Schlafi writes classified sleep data to HealthKit using these standard categories.

Writing Sleep Data

Schlafi creates HKCategorySample entries for each detected sleep stage period. Apple Health aggregates data from all sources. Users see Schlafi's classifications alongside any watch or other tracker data.

On-Device Processing

All computation happens on-device. No cloud, no account, no data upload. Raw accelerometer data is processed into sleep stages and discarded. Only the resulting sleep analysis is stored (HealthKit + app cache).
