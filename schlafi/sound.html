🎤 Sound Science — Passive Microphone Analysis

Phone microphone passively listens during sleep. All audio processing happens on-device — no audio is uploaded, stored, or transmitted. Privacy-first: explicit opt-in with clear disclosure. Audio is processed into features (snoring events, breathing rate, noise levels) and immediately discarded.

Snoring Detection

Strong Evidence — Highest-Confidence Audio Signal

98.2% accuracy, 98.0% sensitivity, 98.3% specificity using directional microphone at 1m distance. AdaBoost classifier with 34 time/spectral features across 76,600+ manually classified episodes.

Source: Kim et al. (2013) “Automatic Detection of Whole Night Snoring Events Using Non-Contact Microphone” — PLOS ONE

Deep neural network approach: 95.3% accuracy, 92.2% sensitivity, 97.7% specificity.

Source: Arsenali B et al. (2021) “Audio-based snore detection using deep neural networks” — PubMed

Snoring occurs in both NREM and REM with OSA-specific patterns. At AHI ≥30: 94–100% sensitivity NREM, 92–97% REM.

Source: Saha S et al. (2018) “Snore Sound Analysis Can Detect the Presence of Obstructive Sleep Apnea Specific to NREM or REM Sleep” — J Clin Sleep Med

Breathing Rate from Audio

Strong Evidence

<1% median error for respiratory rate estimation from phone microphone. 96% of recordings within ±1 bpm in controlled conditions. Lab MAE: 0.2 ± 0.27 bpm.

Effective up to 30 cm from nose.

Remote/noisy conditions: 87.5% within ±1 bpm (MAE: 0.79 ± 2.44 bpm).

Source: Nam Y et al. (2016) “Estimation of Respiratory Rates Using the Built-in Microphone of a Smartphone or Headset” — IEEE J Biomed Health Inform

Limitation: Requires open nasal passages. Nasal congestion causes doubled respiratory rate artifacts. Tracheal sounds produce poor estimates.

Sleep/Wake Detection from Breathing Sounds

Moderate Evidence

83.3% overall accuracy, 92.2% sensitivity (sleep detection), 56.6% specificity (wake detection), Cohen's kappa 0.508. Uses breathing pattern features with AdaBoost classifier. 70 subjects, whole-night validation.

Source: Nakano H et al. (2014) “Sleep-Wake Evaluation from Whole-Night Non-Contact Audio Recordings of Breathing Sounds” — PLOS ONE

Advanced Deep Learning — HomeSleepNet

HomeSleepNet (2023): 76.2% overall accuracy (3-stage: wake/NREM/REM), 88.5% accuracy (2-stage: sleep/wake). Robust across demographics (73.4–79.4% across age, gender, BMI, OSA severity).

Architecture: Mel spectrogram → Listener + Transformer encoder (per-epoch) → Bi-LSTM Transformer for temporal sequence across 40-epoch windows.

Source: Choi SH et al. (2023) “Prediction of Sleep Stages Via Deep Learning Using Smartphone Audio Recordings in Home Environments” — JMIR

Environmental Noise Monitoring

Well-Established

Nocturnal noise disrupts sleep architecture: increased wake and N1, decreased N3 (deep) and REM → shallower sleep overall.

Arousals at ~48 dB (air traffic threshold). Physiological reactions at ~33 dB (increased cortisol, body movements).

Long-term: stress hormone increases, emerging cardiometabolic adverse outcomes.

Source: Basner M & McGuire S (2022) “Environmental Noise and Effects on Sleep: An Update to the WHO Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis” — Environmental Health Perspectives

Sleep Apnea Screening from Audio

Moderate Evidence — Screening Only, Not Diagnosis

Random forest model on breathing sound analysis:

  • AHI ≥5 events/h: 88.2% accuracy, 90.8% sensitivity, 64.7% specificity
  • AHI ≥15 events/h: 82.3% accuracy, 87.3% sensitivity, 70.6% specificity
  • AHI ≥30 events/h: 81.7% accuracy, 83.0% sensitivity, 80.3% specificity
  • Correlation: 0.78 for AHI estimation

Source: Kim J et al. (2022) “Evaluating Prediction Models of Sleep Apnea From Smartphone-Recorded Sleep Breathing Sounds” — PMC

📐 Sound Maths — Audio Processing Algorithms

Snoring Detection Pipeline

Input: Raw audio at 16 kHz sample rate

1. Framing: 25ms frames, 10ms hop (60% overlap)
2. Mel spectrogram: 40 Mel filter banks, 0–8 kHz range
3. Feature extraction per frame:
  • Spectral centroid (frequency center of mass)
  • Spectral rolloff (frequency below which 85% of energy)
  • MFCCs (13 coefficients — Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients)
  • Zero crossing rate
  • RMS energy
4. Classification: Binary (snore/not-snore) per frame
  • On-device CoreML model (trained offline)
5. Aggregation:
  • Snoring Index (SI) = snoring events per hour of sleep
  • Snoring Duration Fraction = total snoring time / total sleep time

Breathing Rate Extraction

Input: Raw audio, 16 kHz

1. Band-pass filter: 0.1–0.6 Hz (6–36 breaths/min range)
  Applied to audio energy envelope, not raw audio
2. Audio energy envelope:
  • Compute RMS energy per 50ms frame
  • Smooth with 1s moving average
3. Band-pass the smoothed envelope to isolate breathing rhythm
4. Peak detection: Count peaks in filtered envelope
  • RR_audio = peaks per minute
5. Per-epoch features:
  • RR_mean = mean respiratory rate over epoch
  • RR_CV = std(RR) / mean(RR) — coefficient of variation

Stage signal:
  RR_CV > 0.15 → irregular breathing → REM indicator
  RR_CV < 0.05 → regular breathing → deep sleep indicator

Audio Sleep/Wake Classifier

Input: 30s audio epochs

1. Convert to Mel spectrogram (same parameters as snoring pipeline)
2. Extract per-epoch features:
  • Breathing regularity (RR_CV)
  • Ambient noise level (RMS energy, dB(A))
  • Movement sound presence (transient high-energy events)
  • Snoring presence (from snoring classifier)
3. Binary classification: sleep vs. wake
  Supplements accelerometer-based sleep/wake detection

Combined with accelerometer:
  P(sleep|audio) augments P(sleep|accel):
  • Breathing detected + low noise → confirms sleep
  • Movement sounds + high noise variance → confirms wake

Noise Level Quality Integration

Continuous monitoring:
  L_eq = 10 × log10(mean(p²/p_ref²)) per epoch
  where p = sound pressure, p_ref = 20 μPa

Arousal correlation:
  Noise spike (>48 dB) + movement spike within 30s → noise-induced arousal event

Quality score modifier:
  Sustained noise >40 dB(A) across >25% of sleep epochs:
    quality_penalty = min(10, (fraction_noisy − 0.25) × 40)
    Applied as: quality = quality − noise_penalty

Noise-induced arousals:
  Each detected arousal → awake time increased proportionally
  (feeds into existing awake% penalty from quality scoring)

Combined Audio + Accelerometer Fusion

Phone-only with sound:
  P(stage) = P_movement × P_cycle × position_bias × M_audio

where M_audio:
  M_REM  = 1.0 + 0.3 × (RR_CV > 0.15)  — irregular breathing → REM boost
  M_deep = 1.0 + 0.2 × (RR_CV < 0.05)  — regular breathing → deep boost
  M_wake = 1.0 + 0.4 × (movement_sounds) — movement sounds → wake boost

Normalize and assign as usual.

Audio adds most value for:
  • REM vs deep (breathing pattern differences)
  • Wake detection (ambient sound changes, movement sounds)
  • Quality context (noise-induced arousals)

🤡 “Possible but Silly” — Active Acoustic Approaches

Ultrasonic Sonar (e.g., ApneaApp, Google Nest Hub approach)

How it works: Phone speaker emits inaudible sound (~20–24 kHz), microphone listens for reflections off chest/abdomen to detect breathing movements.

Lab accuracy: 95–99% for respiratory event detection (University of Washington, 300+ hours vs PSG). Clinical study: 32/37 patients correctly classified (86.5%).

Source: UW ApneaApp

Why it's silly for Schlafi:

  • Requires continuous speaker emission — severe battery drain overnight
  • Phone must be on nightstand facing the user at specific distance — defeats “phone on mattress” premise
  • Environmental interference from pets, ultrasonic pest deterrents, other smart devices
  • Can detect movement but cannot distinguish sleep stages (still = deep OR REM)
  • Privacy: continuous ultrasound emission is detectable and raises concerns
  • Partner in bed creates reflection confusion
Active Acoustic Sensing (audible frequencies)

Emitting audible sound pulses to detect motion from reflections.

Why it's silly: It wakes the sleeper. And their partner. Self-defeating for a sleep app.

Ballistocardiography via Phone Microphone

Concept: Detect heartbeat-induced micro-vibrations (5–50 Hz, micrometer scale) through the mattress.

Source reference: Inan OT et al. (2015) “Ballistocardiography and seismocardiography” — J Appl Physiol

Why it's silly:

  • Phone microphone is electrically isolated from mattress vibrations — no acoustic coupling path
  • Phone on nightstand can't detect mattress-transmitted vibrations
  • Even with mattress contact, motion artifacts from normal tossing/rolling completely dominate the heartbeat signal
  • Would require a dedicated bed sensor, not a phone

📼 Optional Event Recording — Pillow-Style

Two Privacy Tiers

Default mode: Audio is processed into features (snoring events, RR, noise levels) and immediately discarded. No audio stored.

Optional recording mode: When enabled, Schlafi saves short audio clips around detected events (snoring episodes, unusual breathing, loud noises, sleep talking). The user can review these clips the next morning — like Pillow's “Sound Recording” feature.

How It Works

Continuous audio monitoring (always running when mic is enabled):
  1. Real-time classification: snore / breathing anomaly / noise spike / silence
  2. When event detected AND recording is enabled:
    • Save audio clip: 5s before event + event duration + 5s after
    • Tag with: timestamp, event type, confidence, sleep stage (if known)
    • Store locally on device (no cloud)
  3. Morning review screen:
    • Timeline of detected events with playback
    • Event type icons (snoring, noise, talking, etc.)
    • Option to delete individual clips or all clips

Storage:
  • Clips stored in app sandbox (not shared via HealthKit)
  • Auto-delete after configurable retention (default: 7 days)
  • User can export individual clips (share sheet)
  • Estimated storage: ~50 KB per event (compressed audio)
Tier 1 — Analysis Only (default)

Microphone on, audio processed in real-time, features extracted, audio discarded. No clips saved. Minimal privacy footprint.

Tier 2 — Event Recording (opt-in)

Same as Tier 1, plus short clips saved around detected events. Clips stored locally, auto-deleted after retention period. User has full control: review, delete, export.

Why This Matters
  • Users who snore often don't know it. Hearing a recording is far more compelling than seeing a “snoring detected” badge.
  • Sleep talking recordings are a popular feature (entertainment value + partner verification).
  • Environmental noise clips help identify specific disruptions (dog barking, traffic, neighbor's music) — actionable insights.

⚙️ iOS Audio Implementation

Key iOS frameworks and constraints for implementing passive audio sleep analysis.

SoundAnalysis Framework

iOS 13+. Best choice for passive monitoring. Supports custom CoreML models for snoring/breathing classification. Real-time, battery-efficient, on-device processing.

Background Audio

Requires UIBackgroundModes + audio in Info.plist. Persistent notification shown while recording. Explicit microphone permission required.

Hardware Constraints

Phone mic has hardware high-pass filter ~250 Hz (removes some low-frequency breathing detail). Noise floor ~40 dB SPL. Frequency response optimized for 200 Hz–16 kHz (voice range).

Event Recording Storage

Clips in app sandbox using AVAudioRecorder (compressed AAC). Circular buffer approach: continuously record to rolling 10s buffer, flush to file when event detected. Auto-cleanup on retention expiry.
