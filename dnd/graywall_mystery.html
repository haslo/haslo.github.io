Intro Slideshow
Your World You live in a town called Graywall. It's a frontier town in a country of monsters — but the monsters aren't all bad. Your neighbours are goblins, orcs, ogres, and weirder things. Three powerful sisters rule the country from far away. Lately, strange things are happening near town — dark plants growing where they shouldn't, people going missing. There might be a vampire involved. You're going to find out what's going on.
Khorvaire. Your continent. Graywall is on the far western edge. Droaam. The monster nation you call home.

You don't have to know everything about the world, Eberron, or the big war that happened before you were born. If you have questions, just ask — but honestly, your characters wouldn't know most of that stuff either. The world gets bigger as your story goes on.

DM Note The younger one picks first. Once his character exists, send the older one the pitch plus whatever his brother picked — he'll build something complementary on instinct. He already plays D&D and is thinking about DMing, so he'll get it fast. Don't over-brief him — let the brothers figure out their dynamic at the table. The older one can handle a more complex class if he wants one.

Your Characters

Once you've picked race and class, answer these about your character. Both of you.

Tell Your DM Where in town did you grow up?
Bloodstone is the busy market district. The Calabas is the quieter human quarter. Sar Kuraath is the goblin neighbourhood — everything there is tiny.

Who's your best friend?
Could be a goblin, a young gnoll, an orc kid, a halfling cook — you pick.

What's your favourite spot in town?
The arena? The market? A rooftop? The ruins under the city?

What's something you're good at that isn't fighting?
Cooking? Running? Climbing? Fixing things? Talking your way out of trouble?

DM note: Write down their answers. These become plot hooks later. A best friend can be an NPC. A favourite spot can be where they find the first clue. A non-combat skill can save the day when swords won't.

What You Need to Know

This is what you'd know if you grew up in Graywall:

Your neighbours: Goblins, orcs, ogres, gnolls, harpies (they sing the news), gargoyles (they deliver mail), a medusa or two (don't stare). Everyone gets along, mostly. Bigger creatures have the right of way.

The dead: There's a small community near the south gate who follow a faith called the Blood of Vol. They believe everyone has a spark of divinity inside them, and that when you die, your body can still serve the people you love. So they have skeletons and zombies doing the heavy work — hauling stone, standing guard at night, carrying things. It's weird at first, but nobody in Graywall minds. The Seekers (that's what the faithful call themselves) are quiet, they keep to themselves, and their undead don't bother anyone. A few skeletons sweep the streets near the south gate every morning. There's a zombie who tends a candle stall in the Bloody Market — he's been there longer than anyone can remember. In Graywall, the dead are just neighbours who didn't stop working.

Food: There's free stew called grist. It's made from troll meat — the trolls grow it back, so don't feel bad. The best restaurant in town is run by a goblin chef called Bahiri, in the small-people quarter.

The boss: A mind flayer named Xor'chylic runs the town from a tower. Tentacle face, reads minds. He's fair, but you really don't want to annoy him.

The country: Droaam is run by three powerful sisters far away in the capital. They're ancient, they're powerful, and everyone respects them. That's all you need to know about them for now.

What They Don't Need Yet DM Only

Don't explain any of this unless they ask. Even then, give the short version first. If they dig deeper, you can expand — but let them set the pace.

The Last War (894–996 YK) — a century-long war between the Five Nations of Khorvaire over the shattered Galifar kingdom. Ended two years ago with the Treaty of Thronehold. Millions dead. The current year is 998 YK.

DM Note — The War and Droaam Droaam is deeply connected to the war — just not as a battlefield. The Daughters of Sora Kell united the monster races of western Breland around 986 YK and declared Droaam a sovereign nation. Nobody recognised them. But House Tharashk brokered Droaam's monsters as mercenaries to the warring nations — ogre shock troops, troll berserkers, harpy scouts, gargoyle couriers. Droaam profited enormously. Graywall itself was founded by the Daughters as the border gateway, the controlled point of contact between monster nation and human civilisation. The town is only about 12 years old. It's not ancient — it's a startup. If the kids ask about the war, the short version is: "There was a big war between the human countries. It ended a couple of years ago. Droaam sold soldiers to all sides and got rich. Your town was built to make that trade happen."

The Treaty of Thronehold — the peace treaty that ended the Last War. Crucially, it did not recognise Droaam as a legitimate nation. Breland still officially claims the territory. This is a simmering political tension that could matter later in the campaign, but for now it's background noise — nobody in Graywall cares what Breland thinks.

The Mourning (994 YK) — the event that scared everyone into peace. An entire nation (Cyre) was destroyed overnight by an unknown magical catastrophe. Nobody knows what caused it. The former territory is now the Mournland — a dead, warped wasteland. This is the single most important event in recent Eberron history, and it's useful to have in your back pocket because it comes up in every Eberron source. But for the kids, it's a distant horror story — "a whole country just... died one day, and nobody knows why." Don't bring it up unless the campaign moves in that direction.

Dragonmarks / Houses — hereditary magical marks that grant specific powers. Twelve dragonmarked Houses control most commerce and services across Khorvaire (healing, transportation, banking, communication). The relevant one is House Tharashk (Mark of Finding) — they run the Calabas, they broker the monster mercenaries, and they're the closest thing Graywall has to legitimate foreign authority. When the kids encounter Tharashk, the simple version is "they're like a powerful guild that controls trade." The deeper version — that the Houses are essentially supranational corporations with more power than most governments — can wait.

DM Note — Tharashk in Graywall House Tharashk is the intermediary between Droaam and the Five Nations. They have an exclusive contract to broker Droaam's mercenaries and resources. Their compound in the Calabas is a little embassy of civilisation, but they're not naive — Tharashk members working in Droaam tend to be tough, pragmatic, and morally flexible. A Tharashk half-orc who's spent years in Graywall is a very different person from a Tharashk heir in Sharn. Could be a useful NPC — someone who knows both worlds and has complicated loyalties.

The wider world — a kid who grew up in Graywall would have heard of Breland (the big nation next door), Sharn (the enormous city of towers, traders sometimes come from there), and maybe Karrnath or Thrane if they paid attention to travellers' stories. But they wouldn't know or care about the details. Keep it vague: "there are big human cities far to the east, and a dead country nobody goes to." The world becomes real when the story takes them there.

The Daughters of Sora Kell — Sora Katra (the schemer, wears faces), Sora Maenya (the brute, unstoppable), Sora Teraza (the seer, blind and ancient). They rule Droaam from the Great Crag. Every creature in Droaam fears and respects them. The kids know the Daughters exist and that they're in charge — that's the player-facing version. What the kids don't need: the Daughters' long-term political agenda (Droaam recognition, economic independence, military strength), the internal power dynamics between the three sisters, or the fact that Sora Teraza may be playing a much longer game than her sisters realise.

Blood of Vol, the Draconic Prophecy, Overlords — deep Eberron lore. The Blood of Vol is a religion focused on divinity within the self (and sometimes undead immortality). The Draconic Prophecy is a cosmic pattern that dragons and demons have been interpreting for millennia. The Overlords are imprisoned fiendish entities of world-ending power. None of this is relevant until it is. File it under "maybe in six months, maybe never."

DM Note — The Golden Rule If it's not in Graywall and it's not happening to the kids right now, it doesn't exist yet. The world gets bigger as they explore it. Resist the urge to world-build at them — 11-year-olds don't want a lecture, they want to open the next door. Feed the lore through encounters, not exposition.

The Hook Preliminary

Player — Tell Them at Session 1 Dark vines have been growing on the south wall of town. They appeared overnight. The city watch pulled them down, but they grew back by morning. A goblin kid touched one and couldn't feel his fingers for a day. Strange flowers are blooming in the gutters — blood-red, beautiful, and wrong. And a few people who went into the southern hills haven't come back.

Let them investigate. The kids talk to people, follow the vines, find tracks. The trail leads south to an old ruin half-swallowed by dark vegetation. Inside: traps (plants, not mechanical), undead servants, and a garden that shouldn't exist underground — darkwood trees, luminescent flowers, and soil that smells sweet and wrong.

The vampire isn't there yet. First session is the garden, the clues, and the feeling that something intelligent is behind this. They find a journal, or a note, or a beautifully tended greenhouse in the middle of a ruin. Someone is cultivating this. Someone patient.

The Villain Preliminary — Subject to Change

Seriously, Players — Stop Reading This will ruin the story. Your DM has something cool planned and you'll enjoy it more if you don't know what's coming. Go do something else.
DM Eyes Only Inspired by Lord Varonaen Chronicles of Eberron p. 156 — an ancient elven vampire obsessed with plants. He creates hybrid flora using darkwood, blood, and necromantic energy. His "gardens" are alive in the worst way: vines that drain life, roses that charm, trees grown from corpses.

Your version — Lady Verdemis: an old vampire who arrived in the Droaam borderlands decades ago. Presents herself as a travelling scholar — soft-spoken, courteous, endlessly curious about botany. She buys rare seeds from merchants in disguise. She asks about your favourite flowers. She remembers your name. She seems kind.

She is not kind. She is a monster wearing patience like a coat. The garden is fed with blood. The beautiful darkwood trees? Grown from the bodies of the missing travellers — she plants them like bulbs. The luminescent flowers? They thrive on life force drained from living creatures through root systems that spread underground. The vines creeping up Graywall's south wall aren't an accident — they're infrastructure. She's extending her garden into the town, slowly, root by root. The missing people aren't collateral damage. They're compost.

In combat she shifts between two forms: a wraith — incorporeal, flying, draining life on touch — and a darkwood body she animates from plants and trees, lashing out with vines. The wraith is the real her. The darkwood is the weapon. Full stat block in the NPC section.

She knows exactly what she's doing and she enjoys it. But her mask is perfect — gentle, scholarly, a little eccentric. The kind of person you'd invite to dinner. She'd accept. She'd bring flowers. She'd compliment the cooking. And weeks later, something dark and beautiful would bloom in your garden where nothing was planted, and you'd never connect the two.

The Mask: Gentle. Curious. Remembers every name. Always smiling. Hands like ice. Would never raise her voice. The kids should like this NPC at first — the betrayal hits harder that way.

The Truth: Utterly evil. Sees living creatures as raw material. Has been doing this for centuries across different regions — arrives, gardens, leaves when the locals catch on. Droaam is perfect for her because nobody's organised enough to notice a few missing travellers. She considers her work art and the deaths a necessary part of the creative process. Not apologetic. Not conflicted. Just patient.

Weakness 1 — Keeper's Greed Chronicles p. 151 — she must accept any gift freely offered to her. Can't refuse, can't drop it, can't destroy it. Her lair is full of small gifts from people she's killed — that's the clue. In the final fight, the kids offer her something and she has to take it. See the NPC stat block for tactical details.

Weakness 2 — Perfect Etiquette Chronicles p. 151 — when asked a question, she must truthfully answer if her name was spoken as part of that same sentence. The answer can be brief or cryptic. The kids figure out the rule through trial and error ("she dodged the question… but wait, last time I said her name and she answered straight"). Once they know, they can interrogate her in social scenes by weaving "Lady Verdemis" into their questions. She hates it but she can't stop herself.

Escalation: To be planned once we know how much table time we have. The broad arc: strange plants → investigation → the vampire appears → someone goes missing → confrontation.

Vaelk DECOY Goblin Changeling · Con Artist · "The Lord of Südburg"

The Ghost. A goblin with a Dhakaan name and nothing to show for it — "Vaelk" means something like "unseen hand" in old Goblin, and they've made it a lifestyle. Moved into the abandoned castle south of Graywall six months ago because it was free, creepy, and nobody asks questions about a ruin. The lights in the windows, the moving shadows, the plants swinging at night — half of it is Vaelk in different shapes, half is just wind and a bad reputation doing the work for them. Runs a smuggling waypoint through the castle cellar: Droaam antiquities come in, get crated, go out to a contact in the Calabas. Vaelk is Amir in 20 years if Amir never finds a cause.

Decoy logic: The castle is the obvious villain lair — overgrowing, lights at night, creepy as hell. The kids already know about it from session zero. Vaelk is the payoff for going there: not a boss fight, not a vampire, just a panicked changeling in a nightgown who really wishes the kids had knocked. The vines creep Vaelk out too — they burn them off the walls every morning and they grow back by evening. A false lead, but an entertaining one.
The Thesska connection: Vaelk's smuggling route ends at Thesska Mirren's estate. Vaelk is her supplier — Droaam relics through the castle cellar, picked up by her intermediaries at night. Neither knows the other's real name (Vaelk deals as "Lord Draven," she deals through servants). The kids won't notice this until they see matching crate markings or specific artefacts at both locations. Don't point it out. Let them connect it.
Mirror to Amir: Vaelk has the same toolkit as Amir — Deception, disguises, reading people, fast talk. But no oath, no friends, no purpose beyond the next score. Brilliant and completely hollow. If Amir spends time with them, there should be a flash of recognition. Vaelk might genuinely admire the oath — not mockingly, but wistfully. "You actually believe in something. That must be nice."
The witness: Vaelk has been watching the Watching Wood from their tower every night for months. They've seen the vine growth radiate from one point deep in the forest. They know roughly where the epicentre is. They haven't told anyone because attention means losing the castle. They'll trade the information — for protection, a favour, or just someone who doesn't treat them like a freak for being a changeling in Droaam.

The Four Faces

"Vaelk" — goblin male, old by goblin standards, wiry, yellow eyes that miss nothing. The name is ancient Dhakaan — means something like "unseen hand" or "the one who slips through." This is the face Amir meets. Sitting cross-legged on a castle windowsill, eating stolen fruit, grinning like he's been expecting company. Vaelk-as-Vaelk is the con artist teacher: fast-talking, streetwise, genuinely impressed by a kid who took an oath and meant it. He's the one who'll teach Amir to read a room, spot a lie, run a grift without getting caught. Male, because Amir needs someone he can see himself in — and Vaelk sees himself in Amir, which is worse. "You're me, twenty years ago. Before I got smart. Or stupid. Depends on the day."
"Lord Draven" — tall, gaunt human male, silver temples, deep voice. The castle lord. Cold, imperious, exactly what you'd expect from a reclusive noble in a crumbling estate. This is the face Vaelk uses for smuggling deals and the identity Thesska's intermediaries know. If the kids approach the castle formally, Draven answers the door. Dismissive, vaguely threatening, wants them gone. The kids will hate this guy.
"Sister Aldrice" — human female, Silver Flame vestments, soft-spoken, pious. Visits the sick in the Calabas, blesses doorways, quotes scripture. Trusted. Invisible in the way clergy are — nobody questions a priestess. Vaelk finds the irony hilarious. If Sera Tain ever runs into Sister Aldrice, she'll clock the fake immediately — the prayers are slightly wrong, the accent shifts. That's a scene worth setting up.
"Korrga" — orc female, dockworker build, scarred hands, short sentences, long stares. The muscle. Moves crates through Bloodstone at night, handles physical smuggling runs. Nobody messes with Korrga. She's been "working the docks" for months — regulars nod at her. If the kids are watching crate movements through Bloodstone, they'll see Korrga. They will not connect her to Lord Draven or Sister Aldrice unless they get very, very lucky.
The confusion trick: Lord Draven walks into the castle's back room. Korrga walks out. Room's empty. The kids will lose their minds. Don't explain it. Don't even acknowledge it happened. Just describe what they see and let them argue about it.
Personality Charming, slippery, never quite honest. Defaults to humour under pressure. There's no "real face" underneath — the goblin, Draven, Aldrice, and Korrga are all equally Vaelk. They rotate between them the way other people change clothes. If the kids ask which one is the real one: "All of them. That's the point."
The tell When Amir talks about his oath, Vaelk goes quiet. First time anyone's seen them not have a comeback.
Sera Tain Human (Brelish), late 40s · Former Paladin (Oath of Devotion, broken) · "Nothing — officially"

The Charmer. Arrived in Graywall with nothing, acts like she owns the place. Former holy warrior of Thrane who lost her faith when she saw the Silver Flame's crusades up close. Now a grifter in the Calabas — sells "blessed" trinkets, tells fortunes with a straight face.

Silver Flame: Thrane's state religion — a force of genuine good that also ran crusades against lycanthropes and "monsters." The crusades got ugly. That's what broke Sera. E:RftLW p. 144 ExE p. 52
Breland: One of the Five Nations — the big, pragmatic, slightly cynical neighbour to the east. Most humans in Graywall are Brelish. They value independence and common sense over tradition. E:RftLW p. 107

Korrath DECOY Warforged · Circle of the Land Druid · "Warden of the Threshold"

The Preacher. A warforged druid living at the edge of the Watching Wood, south of Graywall. Built during the Last War as a siege unit, found purpose tending wild land after the Treaty. Believes civilisation is a wound that festers — talks about it constantly, loudly, in the market square.

Decoy logic: Publicly warned Graywall "something would come for the walls." Has a ritual clearing in the woods with stone circles and strange moss arrangements. Knows more about local plants than anyone except Verdemis. The kids should suspect him first.
The truth: Genuinely cares about the balance between wild and settled land. Militant about it, but not violent — he protests, he doesn't poison. Has noticed the vine growth and is terrified by it because it's wrong — not natural growth, something forced. Could become an unlikely ally if the party listens past his rhetoric.
The Sermon Incident: Weeks ago, Korrath confronted a lumber merchant cutting trees near the Watching Wood. Shoved him. Town guard intervened. Now everyone remembers his earlier warnings — "something will come for the walls" — and connects dots that don't connect. A shopkeeper near the market can recount the scene in a way that makes Korrath sound unhinged. Great red-herring fuel for an early investigation scene.

Read This First

If you only read one thing before the next session: Chronicles of Eberron — Vampires Chronicles p. 149. That's your villain's engine. Everything else can wait until the kids drag you there organically.

Oath of Self-Made Greatness

Amir's paladin isn't religious or noble. He's ambitious. His oath is personal: I will rise. I will not be ordinary. I will not let others define my worth.

Sera Tain is the catalyst. A broken paladin turned Calabas grifter who still steps forward when it counts. She teaches him a few things — fighting, attitude, how to read people. Then dares him: "If you're going to be great, swear it." He swears. She laughs and walks away. That's the oath origin.

She also teaches him how the oath works — Lay on Hands, the smite, the feeling when the power answers. She knows because she had it and lost it. She can show him the shape of it without being able to do it herself. That's what makes her the right teacher.

This is a story about ambition turning into heroism. Later, challenge Amir: Does greatness mean being feared, or being admired? That's growth fuel.

Paladin & Dragonborn — What Matters

Paladin level 1 PHB p. 108: Lay on Hands — 5 × level HP pool, touch, bonus action, no slot, long rest recharge. Also cures Poisoned (costs 5 HP from pool). Divine Sense — bonus action, detects undead/fiends/celestials within 60 ft for 10 min, Prof Bonus uses/long rest. Weapon Mastery — mastery properties on weapons you're proficient with. Spellcasting — 2 prepared spells + 2 slots at level 1.

Paladin level 2 PHB p. 109: Fighting Style (Defence +1 AC is the safe pick). Paladin's Smite — Divine Smite is a spell, always prepared, doesn't count against your limit. Bonus action after you hit, 2d8 radiant (+1d8 vs undead). Once per turn. One free cast per long rest, then costs a slot. Can't smite on opportunity attacks.

Dragonborn PHB p. 187: Breath Weapon — replaces one Attack action attack, choose cone or line each time, DEX save (DC 8 + CON mod + Prof), 1d10 damage (scales 2d10/3d10/4d10 at 5/11/17). Prof Bonus uses per long rest. Damage Resistance — half damage from breath type, always on. Darkvision 60 ft. Draconic Flight (level 5) — bonus action, sprout spectral wings, fly speed = walking speed, 10 min, 1/long rest.

His turn: Action + Bonus Action + Move. "Hit, then bonus action smite, done" is a perfectly good turn.

Amir's Prepared Spells

Paladin Subclass at Level 3

He picks an oath at level 3 PHB p. 113. Four options in the PHB. For a CHA-focused Charlatan dragonborn played by an 11-year-old:

  • Oath of Glory PHB p. 113 — the pick. Channel Divinity: Peerless Athlete (bonus action, 1 hour, advantage on Athletics + Acrobatics, carry/push/lift doubles). Inspiring Smite shares temp HP with allies after a Divine Smite. Oath spells include Heroism and Guiding Bolt. Fits a kid who wants to be the coolest person in the room — which is exactly what a Charlatan dragonborn is about
  • Oath of Vengeance PHB p. 118 — simple and aggressive. Vow of Enmity (advantage on attacks vs one target, part of Attack action, transfers on kill). Great damage focus. But the oath tenets are dark — relentless pursuit, no mercy. Maybe too edgy for 11
  • Oath of Devotion PHB p. 116 — the classic. Sacred Weapon (10 min, add CHA to attack rolls) is strong with CHA 15. But the tenets are very "be honest, be compassionate" which clashes with Charlatan. Interesting tension if he leans into it, boring if he doesn't
  • Oath of the Ancients PHB p. 115 — nature-themed, resistance to spell damage at level 7. Nature's Wrath (restrain a creature) is fun. But flavour is druidy/fey, doesn't match a street-smart city kid in Graywall

Don't pre-pick. Let him read the oath tenets and choose. But if he asks: Glory.

Breath Type Choice

Dragonborn breath weapon PHB p. 187. Choose 15 ft cone or 30 ft line each time you use it. Always DEX save. Scales: 1d10 → 2d10 (5) → 3d10 (11) → 4d10 (17). Pick a damage type:

  • Fire Gold Brass Red — resistance to fire is the most useful in the game
  • Lightning Bronze Blue — rarely resisted
  • Cold Silver White — solid damage type, good resistance
  • Poison Green — cool flavour but most monsters resist it — worst pick mechanically
  • Acid Copper Black — rarely resisted

Let him pick what sounds cool. Fire or lightning if he wants advice. At level 5 all dragonborn get Draconic Flight — spectral wings, fly speed, bonus action, 1/long rest.

Session 1 Goal

Both kids are at the table. Amir has his character (Dragonborn Paladin/Charlatan) and context from session zero — he knows about the vines and the creepy castle. Kais is new to the world. This session needs to work for both of them: give Amir agency to act on what he already knows, and give Kais a way in that doesn't feel like catching up.

They should meet at least one NPC, get suspicious of something, and end the session with a lead — but not the answer. This is a mystery session, not a combat session. Save the vampire for later.

Opening — Getting Both Kids In

Start with something both brothers experience together. The vines on the south wall have gotten worse overnight — thicker, darker, a section of wall is cracking. A small crowd gathers. People are nervous. This is the inciting incident: the problem has escalated from "weird" to "urgent."

Amir already knows the setup. He'll want to act. Let him be the one who tells Kais's character what's going on — that's better than you recapping. It makes Amir the expert and gives Kais a reason to follow his lead.

Kais needs a reason to care. Either his character lives here too (establish that quickly), or he's just arrived and walks into the scene. Either way: the crowd, the cracking wall, the whispers about missing people — that's enough hook for a 16-year-old. He'll engage.

Then ask: "What do you do?" — and let them decide the direction. Don't steer. The flowchart below handles wherever they go.

The Decoys — Running Them

All decoys are in the NPC section above. Here's how to use them in play:

Korrath (The Preacher) — easiest to encounter. He's in the market square, lecturing anyone who'll listen. The kids hear him ranting about "the walls will fall" and the Sermon Incident gets mentioned by a shopkeeper. He looks guilty. He isn't. If they confront him, he's hostile but honest — and visibly afraid of the vines. A Nature or Insight check (DC 10) reveals his fear is real.

Thesska Mirren (The Recluse) — harder to reach. Locked estate, servants deflect visitors. The tavern gossip route works: former servant Dresh at a Calabas tavern talks about locked rooms and midnight deliveries. Getting inside takes persuasion, deception, or breaking in. Once inside, they find crated antiquities and Cazhaak creepers in the cellar — suspicious but not villainous. She's angry, not scared.

Rixit (The Widow) — the emotional one. Her apothecary in Sar Kuraath is easy to find — anyone in the goblin quarter knows her. She's helpful but evasive. Earning her trust (DC 12 Persuasion, or mention Torvik, or just be kind) unlocks the Map on the Wall — pins radiating south-southwest. That's the real lead.

"I Want to Go to the Castle"

Amir knows about the castle from session zero. It's overgrowing, there are lights at night, shadows moving. He'll want to go there. Let him. This is the smart, brave call — reward it.

What they find: If they knock, Lord Draven answers — imperious, dismissive, wants them gone. If they sneak in or push past Draven, they find a goblin sitting cross-legged on a windowsill eating stolen fruit: Vaelk. Not a vampire, not a boss fight — a panicked con artist in a nightgown who really wishes the kids had knocked. The castle is creepy because Vaelk wants it to be creepy: it keeps people away from their smuggling operation. The vines bother Vaelk too — they burn them off the walls every morning and they grow back by evening.

For Amir: The goblin face is the one that matters. Same skillset — fast talk, disguises, reading people — but no oath, no friends, no purpose beyond the next score. Old Dhakaan blood, ancient name, nothing to show for it but a stolen castle and a good eye for marks. The moment Vaelk goes quiet when Amir mentions his oath is the character beat. "You actually believe in something. That must be nice."

For Kais: Vaelk is a puzzle. A changeling who could be anyone, living in a ruin full of crated artefacts. Kais's character gets to be the one who notices things — the crate markings, the smuggling setup, the fact that Vaelk is scared of the vines too. Give Kais's character the Perception or Investigation checks here.

Don't give the location yet. Vaelk has been watching the Watching Wood from their tower — they've seen the vine growth radiate from one point deep in the forest. But they won't share that in session 1. They don't trust the kids yet. They'll trade information for a favour later — protection, keeping their secret, something. For now Vaelk is a fun encounter and a loose thread. The lead to the Wood should come from the town investigation (Rixit's map, Korrath's knowledge, examining the wall).

If they skip the castle: the vines are all over the south wall but there's no obvious single target. Without information, walking into the Watching Wood is walking blind — miles of monster-filled borderland with no direction. A Survival check (DC 14) gives a partial lead (vines denser to the south-southwest). If they're stuck, have a townsfolk nudge them: "Talk to the goblin herbalist in Sar Kuraath. She's been mapping them."

Introducing Verdemis (mid-phase)

Lady Verdemis first appears mid-phase — session 2 or 3, whenever the investigation is rolling. She doesn't tag along. She shows up irregularly and helps. Two options for the first meeting — pick based on how things went:

Option A — Castle visitor: She shows up at Vaelk's castle, tracking the vine growth to the same area. Vaelk knows her as a regular customer — she buys rare botanical specimens from his smuggling haul. The kids trust her because the goblin trusts her. Ironic. Tragic later.

Option B — The rescue: The kids find her near the vine growth, seemingly overwhelmed by aggressive plants. They save her. She's grateful, "wounded," and now has every reason to stay close. They'll feel personally invested in protecting her — which makes the betrayal hit harder.

After that: she appears when they're stuck. Identifies a plant, offers a theory, then has somewhere else to be. Always helpful, never intrusive. Remembers their names. Each time she turns up feels like a lucky coincidence. It isn't.

Investigation Flowchart

Multiple paths lead to the same place. They don't need to follow all of them — any two should be enough to point toward the Watching Wood.

The castle → find Vaelk → fun encounter, Amir's mirror moment → no location lead yet, but a thread for later
Market square → hear Korrath ranting → confront him → he says the growth is "forced, not natural" and points south
Tavern in Calabas → Dresh gossips about Thesska → investigate her estate → dead end (smuggling, not vines) → but her Cazhaak creepers are unaffected, which is a clue: whatever is growing isn't natural plant life
Sar Kuraath → Rixit's apothecary → earn trust → the Map on the Wall → south-southwest vector confirmed
The south wall → examine vines directly → Nature DC 12: these aren't local species, they're magically accelerated → Herbalism DC 14: faint necrotic trace in the root system

All town roads point to the Watching Wood. Phase 1 ends with them deciding to go there — or already on the path.

Brother Dynamics

A few tools:

Split the spotlight. If one of them does all the talking, have an NPC address the other character directly. "And what about you? You've been quiet." If Kais dominates, give Amir a moment where his specific knowledge matters — he's the one who knows the town, after all.

Let them disagree. If Amir wants the castle and Kais wants to investigate in town, don't pick a side. Ask: "You've got an hour of daylight. Castle or town — what do you do?" Make them negotiate. That's the game.

Give them different strengths. Amir is CHA — he talks to people, reads them, persuades them. Kais's character should get the Perception, Investigation, Arcana checks. They need each other.

Pacing

Phase 1 is 3–6 sessions depending on pacing. For a ~1 hour session with two kids: two encounters max. The opening scene (vines on the wall, crowd) + one NPC encounter or one exploration is a full session. If they go to the castle and meet Vaelk, that's the whole hour. If they stay in town and meet Korrath, that's the whole hour. Don't try to run all the decoys in one go. Let it breathe — spread the decoys, the investigation threads, and the Verdemis introduction across the phase.
