Excited for the tactical combat. Slightly wary of lack of story depth. It could be that there's too many mechanisms here - the entire presentation of the rules (which I meanwhile have) is very "you can do this and that and when you level up you get those and these" ... where things like "what do I actually wear" are abstracted away into "what does that equipment actually do, doesn't matter what kind of cloak and staff it actually is."
So ... not an RPG but a tactical combat game really? Which can be good. Buuut. Is it what I'm looking for?
Presentation is amazing. Such great art. And, the classes look really interesting, too. The classes look to have proper depth, a big investment - right now though it seems like I want something more pick-up-and-play, more ... rough and unhinged? If it's combat. Quick, not agonizing over each turn's multitude of decisions that ultimately are low-stakes because the entire premise of Draw Steel is "I want my power fantasy" ... I'm much more excited about playing a cackling witch that sometimes has things drop out of her cauldron rather than an epic warrior in shining armor that sunders their enemies in a million different ways (but always sunders them). If that makes sense.
So, the system does interest me. But not in a "I want to dive into this right now" kind of way; Vagabond right next to it is much more tempting to read right now.

Cairn 1st Edition (2021, ~48pp pamphlet)
Yochai Gal's rules-light OSR adventure game, combining Into the Odd's auto-hit combat and Knave's inventory-as-class system. Three attributes (STR, DEX, WIL), d20 roll-under saves, HP as “hit protection” that bypasses once depleted (damage goes straight to STR). No classes, no levels — characters defined entirely by what they carry. Spellbooks are items: find one, hold it, cast it. Combat is fast and deadly, exploration is the point. 48 pages, complete game, CC-BY-SA licensed (fully free). Spawned a massive hack ecosystem (Liminal Horror, Runecairn, Mythic Bastionland compatibility). Community-beloved as an ultra-light gateway to the NSR scene.
2nd Edition (2024)
Split into Player's Guide + Warden's Guide. 20 bespoke Backgrounds replace simple d20 character gen — each background is a starting package with gear, a d6 specialisation table, bonds, and world hooks (Electric Bastionland style). Combat initiative reworked (DEX save to act in round 1), spellbook casting formalised (hold in both hands, read aloud, take Fatigue; WIL save if in danger), marketplace items collapsed into flexible groups, dungeon/wilderness exploration procedures tightened. Core rules 100% compatible with 1e content. Box set includes adventure anthology + starter adventure “Trouble in Twin Lakes.”
Minimalist OSR exploration

Not a new edition — a rewrite of the four PF2E core books under the ORC license after the OGL crisis (Jan 2023). Removes all WotC-traceable content. Includes 2023 errata cycle. Legacy books remain compatible.
Player Core (Nov 2023, 464pp)
8 classes (Bard, Cleric, Druid, Fighter, Ranger, Rogue, Witch, Wizard), 8 ancestries. Replaces Core Rulebook. Key changes: ability scores → pure modifiers, alignment → edicts/anathemas + holy/unholy, spell levels → spell ranks, spell schools removed, flat-footed → off-guard, spell components replaced by casting traits, focus refocuses to full natively.
Player Core 2 (Jul 2024, 320pp)
8 classes (Alchemist, Barbarian, Champion, Investigator, Monk, Oracle, Sorcerer, Swashbuckler), 7 ancestries, 40+ archetypes. Replaces Advanced Player's Guide. Champion causes reworked around edicts/anathemas. Alchemist and Oracle getting widest mechanical changes.
GM Core (Nov 2023, 336pp)
Rules, campaigns, Golarion lore, subsystems, variants, magic items, poisons. Replaces Gamemastery Guide. Apex items reworked, staves improved, talismans buffed.
Monster Core (Mar 2024, 376pp)
Replaces Bestiary 1. Tradition-based dragons replace chromatic/metallic as centerpiece. Drow retconned out (replaced by Serpentfolk in Darklands). Renamed: Mephits → Elemental Scamps, Svirfneblin → Drathnelar, Duergar → Hryngar.

CY_BORG (Nov 2022, 160pp digest hardcover)
Cyberpunk Mörk Borg hack by Christian Sahlén, Johan Nohr & Pelle Nilsson (Stockholm Kartell / Free League). Same d20 roll-over chassis, five attributes (Agility, Knowledge, Presence, Strength, Toughness), player-facing combat — enemies never roll. Setting is Cy, a collapsing mega-city with fleshed-out districts (Central, The Hills, The Ports, The Industries), named corps, riot mechanics, and a full NetSys hacking subsystem with nodes/ICE/cyberdecks. Six Punk classes, cyberware as first-class equipment, procedurally-generated apps as tools.
Johan Nohr's layout goes neon-on-void this time — RPGGeek 7.3/10, Wargamer 8/10, "the most visually visceral RPG ever printed" is the running critic line. Same radically open third-party licence as Mörk Borg; huge community ecosystem via Ex Libris CY_BORG and Itch.io zines (HACKiNG_RUN, Down Bad, gutter_PVNKS, Beyond Cy, etc.).
