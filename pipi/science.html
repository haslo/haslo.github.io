🧪 The Science — Hydration Proof Engine

Pipi's Hydration Score isn't guesswork. It's built on peer-reviewed research showing that void frequency is a reliable, validated biomarker of hydration status — and that combining it with intake data gives a clearer picture than either metric alone.

Research Basis

Euhydrated adults void more frequently than hypohydrated adults. Burchfield et al. (2015) found euhydrated subjects averaged 5±2 voids/24h vs 3±1 for hypohydrated subjects. Tucker et al. (2016) confirmed this with 7±2 (euhydrated) vs 5±2 (hypohydrated), with high test-retest reliability (ICC ≈ 0.85) and within-subject coefficients of variation of 15–21%.

Combined, the research places euhydrated void frequency at roughly 5–7 per day. Perrier et al. (2021) further suggests that void frequency, alongside urine color and intake of 2.5–3.5 L/day, can guide daily self-monitoring. However, individual variability means absolute thresholds don't work for everyone. A person who normally goes 5 times is different from someone who goes 9 times. Personal baselines are essential.

Urgency as a Signal

The foundational void-frequency studies (Burchfield et al. 2015; Tucker et al. 2016) standardized on “first urge” voids (perceptual scale 2). Their population norms are therefore in Normal-urgency units. Since urgency maps to bladder fill level and thus voided volume (ICS bladder diary parameters), each urgency level carries a different amount of hydration information.

Why Personal Baselines?

First 7 days, Pipi bootstraps from population averages (6–7 voids/day). As your data accumulates, the score converges to your pattern. Research shows within-subject reliability is high (ICC > 0.85) — meaning your personal average is a stable, meaningful number. Pattern detection takes this further — instead of one personal baseline, Pipi discovers multiple baselines for your distinct day types.

The Health Case — Vasopressin Pathway

Chronic low water intake triggers elevated vasopressin (AVP), measured via its stable proxy copeptin. Elevated copeptin is an independent risk marker for type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, CKD, and cardiovascular disease (Enhörning & Melander 2018).

Increasing water intake by 1–1.5L/day for 6 weeks significantly reduced both fasting copeptin and fasting glucose in habitual low drinkers (Enhörning et al. 2019). At the population level, high water intake correlates with lower triglycerides and higher HDL, with copeptin as the unifying variable (Brunkwall et al. 2020).

Pipi doesn't measure copeptin — but the behavioral inputs it tracks (water intake, void frequency) are the same practical proxies the research recommends for daily self-monitoring.

🧠 The Intelligence — Pattern Detection

Most apps treat every day the same. Pipi learns that your Thursday is different from your Saturday. An on-device pattern engine clusters your days into natural types — no labels needed, no cloud, no creepiness. It just makes everything smarter.

The Honest Approach

Rather than throwing k-means at 14 days of data and hoping for the best, Pipi uses a tiered system that earns its complexity. Day-of-week grouping provides value from week one. Data-driven clustering only activates once there's enough data to be meaningful. The best algorithm is the simplest one that actually works.

Three Phases — Earning Intelligence

1
Day-of-Week Baselines
Week 1+ — From day 7, group data by day-of-week and compute hourly average profiles. Your average Monday, your average Saturday. Zero ML, fully interpretable, robust immediately. Research confirms weekday/weekend is the dominant signal for daily consumption patterns. Already enough to stop 6am Sunday reminders.
See Maths tab for algorithm details
2
Data-Driven Clustering
Day 30+ — Reduce each day to 4–5 features (morning, afternoon, evening, night totals + daily total). K-means (k=2–4) with k-means++ init on reduced features. Gap statistic validates whether real clusters exist — k=1 (“your days are consistent”) is a valid answer. Silhouette score picks optimal k. 30-day rolling window handles life changes.
See Maths tab for algorithm details
3
Adaptive Intelligence
Day 60+ — Clusters get auto-named by characteristics (“Morning Loader”, “Steady Sipper”). Real-time partial-day matching: compare observed hours only, weighted by completeness (min 6 hours before attempting). Anomaly detection: >2σ from any centroid = gently flagged unusual day. Re-clusters weekly as data evolves.
See Maths tab for algorithm details
Why This Tiered Approach?

Published clustering guidelines (Dolnicar et al., Qiu & Joe) show that 24-dimensional k-means needs 500–700+ samples for stable centroids. 14 days of data in 24D = centroid instability, overfitting to noise, forced patterns in randomness.

Dimensionality reduction (to 4–5 features) + gap statistic validation = robust clustering with just 30 days. Day-of-week grouping provides immediate value while the ML phase accumulates enough data to be honest.

What It Feels Like

The algorithm is invisible. You never see phases or clusters or vectors. You just notice that your Sunday reminders come later than your Monday ones, that the app somehow knows you drink less on weekends, that it stops nagging at 6am when you're not at the office. It just… knows you.
