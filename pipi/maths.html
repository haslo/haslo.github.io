📐 Hydration Score — Mathematical Specification

The Hydration Score (0–100) combines three independently computed subscores. Each is designed to be meaningful at any time of day, converge to personal norms over time, and degrade gracefully with sparse data. See the Science tab for the research basis.

Design Rationale

The 120% cap prevents gaming (drinking excessive water for score) while rewarding a healthy buffer above goal. Hitting your exact goal earns 83% of the subscore; the remaining 17% rewards a modest surplus. Score is linear — no cliffs, no bonus thresholds. Research (Perrier 2021) recommends 2.5–3.5 L/day, so the default goal of 2.5L with a 120% cap covers this range.

A2: Frequency Score (0–30)

30
Frequency

Urgency Weights — Volume-Proxy Derivation

The Burchfield/Tucker studies standardized on “first urge” voids (perceptual scale 2 = Normal). Their population norms (eu=5±2, eu=7±2) are therefore in Normal-equivalent units. Weights are derived from typical voided volumes reported in ICS bladder diary parameters and functional bladder capacity studies (~400–500ml):

EmojiUrgencyPerceptual ScaleTypical VolumeWeight (vol / 250ml)
😌Just in Case0–1 (no/weak urge)~100 ml0.4
😊Normal2 (first urge)~250 ml1.0
😰Urgent3–4 (strong/desperate)~350 ml1.4

Why volume-proxy weights work: Higher urgency = fuller bladder = more urine volume per void = stronger evidence of kidney throughput. A single Urgent void produces ~1.4× the volume of a Normal void, so it carries ~1.4× the hydration signal. Conversely, a Just-in-Case void (going before a meeting, not because you need to) produces only ~0.4× the volume — it's a behavioral event, not a strong physiological signal. Because the research baselines were measured at Normal urgency, these weights convert any urgency back to “Normal-equivalent voids” — the unit the population norms are expressed in.

Formulas

Veff = Σ w(ui)  —  effective void count (urgency-weighted sum)

B = 6.0  —  bootstrap baseline (days 1–7)
    rolling_7d_mean(Veff)  —  personal baseline (day 8+)

Why 80% Threshold?

No penalty for exceeding expected count. Research consistently shows higher void frequency = better hydrated. The 80% threshold is intentionally generous because within-subject variability is real (Tucker 2016, ICC > 0.85 but not 1.0). You need to be noticeably below your baseline to lose points.

A3: Throughput Score (0–30)

30
Throughput

Algorithm — Water-Void Matching

For each water event j at time tj with volume vj:

  1. Eligibility:   eligiblej = 1 if (now − tj) ≥ 3h
     (full response window must have elapsed)

  2. Response window:   [tj + 0.5h,   tj + 3h]
     (kidneys need ≥30 min; most response within 3h)

  3. Match:   matchedj = 1 if ANY void exists within the response window

Score calculation:
  If Σ(eligiblej × vj) > 0:
    R = Σ(eligiblej × matchedj × vj) / Σ(eligiblej × vj)
    Sthroughput = R × 30
  Else:
    Sthroughput = 15  —  neutral (not enough time has passed to judge)

Design Rationale

Volume-weighted matching means missing a 500ml bottle of water matters more than missing a small sip. The 3-hour eligibility gate prevents penalizing recent intake (you just drank — give your kidneys time). The 0.5h–3h response window is based on normal kidney processing time: Perrier et al. (2013) showed strong correlation between fluid intake and subsequent urine output within this timeframe. The neutral default of 15 (half marks) ensures the score isn't punished early in the day when no water events are old enough to evaluate.

1
Bootstrap (Days 1–7)
B = 6.0 effective voids/day
Source: Midpoint of Burchfield (eu mean = 5) and Tucker (eu mean = 7). Combined euhydrated range: roughly 5–7 voids/day.

All voids weighted equally until enough data for personal calibration.
2
Rolling Average (Day 8+)
B = mean(Veff[day] for day in last_7_days)
7-day window smooths day-to-day variation. ICC > 0.85 (Tucker 2016) confirms personal averages are stable.
3

B = mean(Veff[day] for day in same_cluster ∩ last_30_days)
If pattern detection is active and current day classified, baseline comes from days in the same cluster. Otherwise falls back to rolling average.

Pattern-aware baselines mean a “Weekend Sipper” day isn't judged against “Weekday Worker” norms.

🧠 Pattern Detection — Algorithm References

Established Methods Only

Pattern detection uses exclusively established statistical and machine learning methods. Pipi invents no new algorithms — it applies well-studied techniques to hydration data. This section documents the specific methods and their academic references. See the Science tab for the product experience these algorithms enable.

C1: Day-of-Week Baselines (Day 7+)

1
Day-of-Week Baselines
Method: Group-by aggregation (descriptive statistics)

For each day_of_week d ∈ {Mon, Tue, ..., Sun}:
  profiled = mean(hourly_intake[day] for day where weekday(day) = d)

Standard deviation computed per day-of-week for anomaly context.
Reference

Day-type classification of consumption profiles is standard in load-profile analysis (ScienceDirect). Weekday/weekend split is consistently the dominant signal.

Why it works immediately: No parameters to tune, no minimum sample size beyond 7 days, fully interpretable. Already enough to differentiate “your average Monday” from “your average Saturday.”

C2: K-Means Clustering (Day 30+)

2
Data-Driven Clustering

Feature Extraction

For each day, compute 4 features:
  fmorning   = water intake 06:00–12:00 / daily_total
  fafternoon = water intake 12:00–17:00 / daily_total
  fevening   = water intake 17:00–22:00 / daily_total
  fnight     = water intake 22:00–06:00 / daily_total

Normalization to fractions (sum = 1.0) removes volume differences,
isolating the temporal pattern.

K Selection — Gap Statistic

For k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4}:
  1. Run k-means++ on feature matrix X (30 days × 4 features)
  2. Compute Wk = within-cluster sum of squares
  3. Generate B reference datasets from uniform distribution over feature range
  4. Compute Gap(k) = (1/B) Σ log(W*kb) − log(Wk)
  5. Compute sk = std(log(W*kb)) × √(1 + 1/B)

Select smallest k where: Gap(k) ≥ Gap(k+1) − sk+1

k = 1 is valid (“your days are consistent”).
Gap Statistic Reference

Tibshirani R, Walther G, Hastie T (2001). “Estimating the number of clusters in a data set via the gap statistic.” J. R. Statist. Soc. B, 63(2), 411–423.

Cluster Validation — Silhouette Score

For each point i:
  ai = mean distance to other points in same cluster
  bi = min(mean distance to points in each other cluster)
  si = (bi − ai) / max(ai, bi)

Mean silhouette S̄ = mean(si for all i)
Accept clustering if S̄ > 0.25 (weak structure or better).
Silhouette Reference

Rousseeuw PJ (1987). “Silhouettes: a graphical aid to the interpretation and validation of cluster analysis.” J. Comput. Appl. Math., 20, 53–65.

K-Means++ Initialization

1. Choose first centroid uniformly at random from data points
2. For each remaining centroid:
  a. Compute D(x) = distance to nearest existing centroid for each point x
  b. Choose next centroid with probability proportional to D(x)²
3. Repeat until k centroids chosen
Then run standard k-means (Lloyd's algorithm) to convergence.
K-Means++ Reference

Arthur D, Vassilvitskii S (2007). “k-means++: The advantages of careful seeding.” Proc. 18th annual ACM-SIAM symposium on discrete algorithms, 1027–1035.

Sample Size

Dolnicar et al. (2014) recommend N ≥ 10·d·k for stable k-means. With d=4 features and k≤4: N ≥ 160 ideally. A 30-day window is the minimum viable — reduced dimensionality (4 vs 24 features) makes this tractable but results improve with more data.

C3: Adaptive Classification (Day 60+)

3
Adaptive Intelligence

Real-Time Day Classification

Given today's partial observations xpartial (hours 0..h):
  1. Truncate each centroid to the same hours: cpartial
  2. Compute Euclidean distance: dk = ||xpartial − ck_partial||
  3. Classify: assigned_cluster = argmink(dk)
  4. Confidence: 1 − (dassigned / Σ dk)

Minimum 6 hours of data before attempting classification.

Anomaly Detection

If dassigned > 2 × σcluster (within-cluster std dev):
  Flag as unusual day (no action, just informational).

Auto-Naming

Clusters receive auto-generated labels based on their centroid's dominant time block:
  • Highest fmorning → “Morning Loader”
  • Balanced fractions → “Steady Sipper”
  • Highest fevening → “Evening Catch-Up”
  • etc.

Maintenance

Re-cluster weekly using 30-day rolling window.
Life changes (new job, season change) naturally phase out old patterns.
